The Evolution

40 years of Critical Illness cover

From a South African surgeon's idea to today's 182-condition policies — how CI has transformed.

1983 · South Africa
The Invention
Dr Marius Barnard (brother of heart transplant pioneer Christiaan) creates "dread disease" insurance after seeing patients survive serious illness but face financial ruin.
1986 · United Kingdom
UK Launch
Abbey Life and Cannon Lincoln launch the first UK CI policies. Early products cover just 4–6 conditions: heart attack, stroke, cancer, kidney failure, coronary bypass, major organ transplant.
1990s · Expansion
Market Growth
Major insurers enter the market. Condition counts grow to 10–20. MS, Parkinson's, blindness, and deafness added. Policies remain inconsistent — every insurer uses different definitions.
2007 · Standardisation
ABI Statement of Best Practice
The Association of British Insurers publishes model wordings for core conditions. For the first time, "heart attack" and "cancer" mean roughly the same thing across providers.
2014 · Innovation
Severity-Based CI Arrives
Vitality launches the UK's first severity-based CI. Instead of all-or-nothing, policies now pay 25%, 50%, or 100% depending on condition severity. Multiple claims become possible.
2019 · ABI Update
Heart Attack Definition Modernised
ABI updates model wordings to reflect advances in cardiac diagnostics. Troponin testing replaces outdated enzyme tests. Many older policies still use pre-2019 definitions.
2022 · ABI Update
Cancer & Stroke Definitions Updated
ABI revises cancer staging and stroke definitions. Modern policies now cover conditions that would have been declined 10 years ago. The gap between old and new policies widens.
2026 · Today
182 Conditions, 27+ Providers
The best policies now cover conditions unimaginable in 1986: FND, severe endometriosis, CFS/ME. Children's CI included free. Claims rates exceed 91%. But many clients still hold outdated policies.
